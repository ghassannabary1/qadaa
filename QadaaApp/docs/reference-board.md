# Qadaa Visual Reference Board

## Goal

Build a calm, premium visual system for Qadaa inspired by Islamic sacred decoration, especially the Dome of the Rock interior, without turning the app into dense wallpaper.

The design direction should feel:
- sacred, not theatrical
- ornamental, not cluttered
- warm, not loud
- premium, not generic
- focused enough for a daily habit app

## Core Principle

Use Islamic decoration as a visual language, not as literal full-screen art.

The app should rely on:
- one header-only ornamental hero treatment
- one secondary floral accent system
- one tiny divider or badge ornament family
- disciplined spacing and card structure

Avoid:
- full-screen dense pattern fields
- literal tile replicas behind content
- bright gold everywhere
- decoration behind dense stats, rows, or calendars

## Reference Families

### 1. Dome Of The Rock Interior Mosaics

Use for:
- sacred atmosphere
- gold / green / blue-green palette logic
- vegetal rhythm
- jewel-like accents

Why it fits:
- recognizably Islamic
- rich but ordered
- ideal for the top header zone

References:
- [Khan Academy: Dome of the Rock](https://www.khanacademy.org/humanities/ap-art-history/west-and-central-asia-apahh/west-asia/a/the-dome-of-the-rock-qubbat-al-sakhra)
- [The Met on the Dome of the Rock](https://www.metmuseum.org/zh/exhibitions/listings/2012/byzantium-and-islam/blog/where-in-the-world/posts/dome-of-the-rock)
- [Wikimedia Commons: Interior of the Dome of the Rock](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Interior_of_the_Dome_of_the_Rock)

What to borrow:
- sacred symmetry
- vegetal scroll feeling
- restrained jewel-like accent shapes

What not to copy literally:
- dense all-over mosaic coverage
- high saturation gold-and-blue fields

### 2. Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque Interior

Use for:
- softness
- dome-centered calm
- radial composition
- meditative hero treatment

Why it fits:
- gentler than monument-heavy references
- better for a daily-use mobile app

References:
- [IRCICA: Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque](https://www.islamicarchitecturalheritage.com/listings/sheikh-lotfollah-mosque)

What to borrow:
- centered medallion / dome logic
- quiet symmetry
- ornamental framing around content

### 3. Illuminated Manuscript Frontispieces And Shamsa

Use for:
- hero card hierarchy
- progress-card composition
- framed content treatment
- premium editorial atmosphere

Why it fits:
- helps translate ornament into interface hierarchy
- useful for hadith and progress blocks

References:
- [Library of Congress illuminated frontispiece](https://www.loc.gov/item/2019714617/)
- [Qatar Museums illuminated shamsa](https://collections.qm.org.qa/en/objects/illuminated-shamsa-blazon-from-a-royal-album-of-shah-jahan-ms532007)

What to borrow:
- centered emblem feeling
- border logic
- disciplined ornament-to-content relationship

### 4. Iznik Floral Tiles

Use for:
- floral accents
- corners
- dividers
- micro-ornament

Why it fits:
- floral without becoming loose or messy
- naturally supports green, muted gold, terracotta, cream

References:
- [Aga Khan Museum Iznik tile](https://collections.agakhanmuseum.org/collection/artifact/tile/872)
- [Art Institute of Chicago: Pair of Tiles with Floral Design](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/72850/pair-of-tiles-with-floral-design)
- [Fitzwilliam Museum Iznik floral tile](https://data.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/id/image/media-200451)

What to borrow:
- saz leaves
- tulip / carnation discipline
- curved floral edge accents

What to avoid:
- repeated floral wallpaper behind all content

### 5. Topkapi Geometry

Use for:
- spacing discipline
- card logic
- dividers
- modular pattern thinking

Why it fits:
- makes the product feel intentional, not merely themed

References:
- [Getty: The Topkapi Scroll](https://www.getty.edu/publications/virtuallibrary/9780892363353.html)
- [The Met: Geometric Patterns in Islamic Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/geometric-patterns-in-islamic-art)

What to borrow:
- geometry as structure
- repetition with restraint
- layout order beneath ornament

## Recommended Motif System

### Primary Motif

`Faded Medallion`

Description:
- one large cropped medallion in the Home header
- dome / octagon / shamsa-inspired
- mostly linework
- low-opacity

Use:
- Home header
- onboarding hero

### Secondary Motif

`Vegetal Mosaic Veil`

Description:
- scrolling floral or vegetal layer
- tone-on-tone
- only on one zone, not full-screen

Use:
- hadith card backdrop
- onboarding supporting surface
- empty states if needed

### Accent Motif

`Jewelled Crown / Rosette`

Description:
- very small accent ornament
- badge, divider, tab highlight, or section marker

Use:
- dividers
- mini labels
- tab highlights

## Placement Map

### Home
- Header hero: yes
- Hadith card: yes, subtle
- Progress card: maybe a tiny accent only
- Quick action card: mostly clean
- Prayer rows: no wallpaper

### Onboarding
- Hero ornament: yes
- Setup cards: minimal

### History
- Calendar grid: no wallpaper
- Maybe a tiny accent on the month header only

### Q&A
- Divider or tiny ornament only

### Settings
- Minimal or none

## Palette Direction

Primary colors:
- deep teal / blue-green
- dark green
- warm parchment / cream
- muted gold

Accent colors:
- soft terracotta / rust in very small amounts
- rose-gold only as a light highlight

Rules:
- the wallpaper should feel almost monochrome at first glance
- bright gold is for emphasis, not large background fills
- terracotta should be tiny and rare

## Typography Direction

Recommended system:
- Arabic body/UI: `Noto Naskh Arabic`
- Arabic accent: `Amiri`
- English body/UI: `IBM Plex Sans`
- English editorial accent: `Source Serif 4`

Usage:
- keep typography quieter than ornament
- use elegant accent fonts sparingly

## Shipping Guardrails

Do:
- keep ornament in header zones
- crop motifs instead of tiling them fully
- maintain strong readability
- keep one main motif family at a time

Do not:
- put dense pattern behind all content
- use multiple competing motif families at once
- animate wallpapers
- cover the calendar or prayer rows in ornament
